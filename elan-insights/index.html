Élan Year Plan, and what other clinics teach us

Learnings from UK clinics we study while building websites. Each new clinic adds a card below and a line in the update log.

Summary in 60 seconds

  • The idea: sell Élan patients a 12-month plan, paid monthly, that covers what weight loss does to the face, skin and hair, not just the medication.
  • Why Élan can win: any clinic can prescribe GLP-1. Only Élan can time skin and hair care to the patient's weight curve. A beauty clinic has no weight data.
  • Proof it works elsewhere: Sure Aesthetics (Surrey) runs a yearly VIP plan with monthly payments; clients say it is why they stay for years. The Nantwich Clinic (Cheshire) sells skin, maintenance and GP care as memberships, with a cheaper maintenance plan after the first course. Hardwick (Cambridge) and Portland (Cardiff) already market "skin after weight loss".
  • Positioning: "Lose the weight. Keep your face." Narrow and ownable. Every beauty service on the menu should answer a problem weight loss causes.
  • Don't: launch a new price ladder (join Lose, Maintain and the new skin services instead), hide the offer, or market medicine names.
  • First step: a 60-day test with current and recent Élan patients, no new equipment. One number decides it: the share of weight-loss patients who take a plan that includes skin or hair care.

The Élan Year Plan

1. The problem we solve

Rapid weight loss predictably brings three visible changes. Patients will face them whether or not we treat them:

  • Hair shedding, often 2 to 4 months after rapid loss begins (telogen effluvium). Usually temporary, very distressing.
  • Facial volume loss, the "Ozempic face" in the press. It is fat loss, not a drug side effect.
  • Loose skin on the face, neck and body, most visible once weight stabilises.

2. The plan follows the weight curve, not the calendar

PhaseWhenFocus
1. StartMonths 0 to 2Baseline photos of face, skin and hair. Skin-quality support begins. Nutrition and protein for hair.
2. Active lossMonths 2 to 6Hair support before shedding usually starts. Gentle skin-quality treatments during loss.
3. StabiliseMonths 6 to 9Volume and lifting treatments once weight settles.
4. MaintainMonths 9 to 12+Maintenance sessions. Review results against baseline photos. Renew the plan.

Hardwick Clinic gives the same clinical advice publicly: wait for weight to stabilise before volume treatments such as Sculptra, but skin-quality treatments can start earlier. Source

3. What the plan includes

  • A written 12-month plan handed over at the first consultation
  • The same lead clinician throughout (continuity is what patients pay for)
  • Scheduled treatments by phase, plus baseline and progress photos
  • Priority booking and first access to new treatments (cheap to give, feels premium)
  • One monthly payment

4. How it fits the current Élan prices

Élan already sells in three steps on elanclinic.ee: Start (initial consultation €150), Lose (active weight management €300 per 3 months) and Maintain (weight maintenance €150 per 3 months). "Skin during weight change" and Élan Aesthetics open on 1 November 2026.

Recommendation: no new price ladder. The Year Plan is a way of joining what already exists: the Lose and Maintain packages plus the new skin and aesthetics services, planned across 12 months from the first consultation. Two options worth testing:

  • Skin add-on: an optional skin-and-hair add-on to the Lose and Maintain packages, timed to the weight curve.
  • Year Plan as one choice: Lose plus Maintain plus a set number of skin visits, shown as one yearly plan with one monthly figure. Transform (UK) shows that bundling the aftercare as standard makes patients see maintenance as part of the treatment.

Final structure and prices are Eva-Maria's decision.

5. The 60-day test

  1. Offer the Year Plan only to current and recent Élan weight-loss patients.
  2. Use two or three treatments that need no new equipment.
  3. Track one number: the share of weight-loss patients who take a plan that includes skin or hair care.
  4. Roughly above 20 percent: build it out. Under 10 percent: park it. These thresholds are starting assumptions, not market data.

Clinic insights

Each card: what the clinic does, what Élan can take, and what to avoid. Links open the clinic's own public pages.

Aftercare modelRochdale, UK

Transform (weight loss surgery)

What they do: one of the UK's largest weight-loss surgery groups. Every weight-loss procedure includes a year's free membership to their online support hub (recipes, meal plans, expert guides) plus access to specialist nurses and dietitians. Free virtual first consultation. BMI checker on the homepage.

"#LifeBeyondTheWeight"

"It is the aftercare that really makes all the difference."

Take: bundle 12 months of maintenance support into the treatment as standard, not as an upsell. It frames maintenance as part of the treatment, and patients used to support tend to renew. A written support hub (recipes, plans, guides) scales cheaply; Élan's Materjalid hub is a start.

Avoid: surgery-first framing and "slimmer you" language. Élan's tone should stay medical and calm.

Best membership modelCheshire, UK

The Nantwich Clinic

What they do: a medical, aesthetic and health hub (GP, physio, skin, body) that sells almost everything as a membership: Skin Gym tiers (five levels, from facials to microneedling courses, with skin analysis every 4 to 6 weeks), Aesthetic Maintenance Plans (only after a completed course, only if a clinician confirms it adds value), and a 12-month Preventative GP Membership built around a baseline health assessment.

"Your skin's monthly personal trainer."

"The best aesthetic results are not achieved once, they are managed intelligently over time."

Take: three ideas straight into Élan. (1) Two-stage logic: an initial course first, then a cheaper maintenance plan, which is exactly Élan's "lose, then keep" story. (2) Baseline first: every plan starts with an assessment and is reviewed against it; Élan can do this with weight, body composition, skin and hair photos. (3) Clinician gatekeeping: "maintenance is recommended only where it genuinely adds value" builds trust and protects the premium.

Avoid: five skin tiers is too many choices; Élan should keep two or three. Their menu also spans hearing tests to pelvic floor, which works for a big hub but would blur a focused clinic.

Most usefulSurrey, UK

Sure Aesthetics, Capel

What they do: a yearly "VIP Club" treatment plan, cost spread monthly, priority booking, first access to new technology, same senior practitioner. Free consultation first.

"Long-term skin improvement, not quick fixes."

"You are not paying for what you do not need, while also avoiding under-treatment."

Take: sell a year, not a session. Make the consultation produce a written plan. Make continuity the product.

Avoid: their best offer is buried in a blog post with no price. Booking means phoning. The menu is very broad (laser hair removal, permanent make-up, veins), which blurs the brand.

Closest to ÉlanCambridge, UK

Hardwick Clinic

What they do: doctor-led clinic with a dedicated page on GLP-1 weight loss and skin. Plain-language FAQs: Will my face look gaunt? Before or after weight loss? Body skin too? How many sessions?

Take: the FAQ format, answering the patient's actual fear in their words. The timing rule: volume after weight stabilises, skin quality earlier. Also: they link aesthetics with nutrition advice.

Watch: proof that UK clinics already target this patient. The idea is real, and the window to own it in Estonia will not stay open forever.

Body focusCardiff, UK

Portland Body Clinic

What they do: a "Post weight loss" section on the homepage.

"The weight has gone. What's next?"

Take: organise the menu by concern, not by treatment name ("Start with your concern"). Patients don't know treatment names; they know what bothers them. The headline above is a model for Élan's tone.

Avoid: leading with a price hook ("From £99") and a % fat-loss claim. That pulls toward discount body contouring, the opposite of a premium medical brand.

Do and don't for Élan

Do
  • Sell a 12-month plan, paid monthly
  • Time care to the weight curve
  • End every consultation with a written plan
  • Show "from €X per month" clearly on the site
  • Organise services by concern: hair, face, skin, body
  • One-tap booking
  • Use recent reviews and before-and-after photos (with consent) as proof
Don't
  • Build a new, separate price ladder
  • Hide the plan inside articles
  • Add services unrelated to weight loss (laser hair removal, permanent make-up)
  • Lead with discount prices or percent claims
  • Name prescription medicines in marketing
  • Buy equipment before the 60-day test proves demand
